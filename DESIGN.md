# Design: Fifth Gear Fitness (solo workout coach)

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-11-09
Status: DRAFT
Mode: Builder (personal tool)

## Problem Statement

Sarah does Fifth Gear Fitness-style workouts on her own, without a studio coach. Nobody runs the clock or calls the blocks. On top of that, since the latest iOS update the built-in Clock is hard to reach: the Control Center shortcut opens a small timer view instead of the full app. Mid-set, that's too many taps.

## What Makes This Cool

One tap on the home screen and the clock is running. The app then acts as the coach: it hands her today's workout, says each interval out loud, and saves what she did.

## Constraints

- Just for Sarah: no accounts, no App Store, no social features.
- It's a web app added to the iPhone home screen. Next.js, deployed to Vercel like her other apps.
- Xcode isn't installed, and native iOS isn't the plan for v1.
- iPhone web apps can't vibrate (Safari doesn't support it). Callouts have to be sound and spoken voice.
- Callouts only play reliably while the screen is on, so the app keeps the screen awake during a workout.

## Premises

1. Getting from the home screen to a running clock in one tap matters most. Everything else comes after that.
2. Intervals are announced out loud, so she never has to look at the screen while she's on the tread.
3. "Today's workout" starts as a small hand-written set of Fifth Gear Fitness-style templates (tread, row, floor blocks). No AI workout generation in v1.
4. It's personal: workout history stays on her phone. No login.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Home-screen web app (CHOSEN)
Next.js on Vercel, added to the home screen. One tap opens it full screen. It keeps the screen awake and works offline. Downside: it can't show the timer on the lock screen, and sounds may pause if the phone locks. Effort: small.

### Approach B: Native iPhone app
Gets a lock-screen timer, Dynamic Island, an Action button launch, and a Control Center button that opens the full app. Downside: it needs Xcode plus the $99/yr Apple Developer Program to keep it installed, and it's more build time. Effort: medium to large.

## Recommended Approach

Approach A. It fixes the one-tap problem this week with tools Sarah already ships with. If the lock-screen gap turns out to bother her at the gym, the workout templates and log carry over to a native version.

## How it works (v1)

1. **Stopwatch (home screen):** big Start/Stop, Lap, and Reset buttons. Time is worked out from the start time, so it stays right even if she switches apps or the screen sleeps.
2. **Today's workout:** pick a template and it's laid out as blocks (for example, tread 25 min, row 10 min, floor 15 min) with each interval listed.
3. **Interval runner:** a beep plus a spoken callout at every change ("Push pace, 30 seconds", "Switch to rower"). Big countdown on screen. The screen stays awake.
4. **Log:** at the end, save the date, the template, and optional notes (distance, weights). The history list lives on her phone.

## Open Questions

- Which workouts go in the first template set? Sarah's memory of real Fifth Gear Fitness classes beats anything invented.
- Does she want the voice, the beeps, or both? Will she be wearing headphones?
- Does the log need numbers per block (tread distance, row meters) or just one notes box?

## Success Criteria

- From the home screen, one tap, and the clock is running in under 2 seconds.
- A full 60-minute session runs start to finish without the clock drifting or the callouts stopping.
- She uses it for at least 3 real workouts in the first two weeks.

## Distribution Plan

Deploy to Vercel. On her iPhone: open the URL in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. No App Store.
